# Floating map posts

Use the map's “게시물” button or + to share text, a photo, or a YouTube video. Select a map location after composing. Cards are anchored to normalized world coordinates and stay at that point when the camera moves. The list opens cards outside the current viewport. Detail dialogs use a portal above room controls; videos mount only after pressing play and unmount on close. Playback is individual, not synchronized watch-party playback.

The author can drag a card with mouse or touch to move it precisely, or choose a new map location in its detail dialog. A five-pixel threshold distinguishes clicks from dragging; dragging preserves the grab offset, samples the current world rectangle to account for camera position, previews locally and publishes the final position on release. Cancel/lost capture restores the original position. Every room member can toggle six reactions and add comments. Opening a detail dialog records a unique viewer identity and last-viewed time, shown under “본 사람”. Text is rendered as text, never HTML. YouTube URLs are parsed against exact known hosts and only the video ID is embedded. Raster uploads are decoded and re-encoded as JPEG at up to 1000 px and 180 KB of encoded data; SVG is not accepted. GIF uploads become still images.

Room sharing uses LiveKit reliable data packets and the existing chunk reassembly. Map packets use 7000-character chunks to bound multibyte UTF-8 packet size. Direct mutations validate the sender identity; author-only operations, timestamps, coordinates, content lengths, comment limits and reaction allowlists are checked. Stable LiveKit identities retain authorship across nickname changes. These are peer-shared room data, not a server-moderated persistent database.

On `REQUEST_SLOTS`, one existing participant elected by identity sends snapshots directly to the newcomer. Snapshots merge comments, viewer records and latest per-user reactions rather than replacing state, and deletion tombstones prevent stale snapshots reviving deleted cards. Briefly out-of-order mutations wait for their base post. Failed local transmissions expose a retry button.

IndexedDB (`voiceroom-map-posts`, room keys in `rooms`) persists posts, images, positions, comments, reactions and viewers on each browser. Joining restores that room's cache and broadcasts the restored snapshot so returning participants can contribute missing data. Cache writes merge transactionally with the previously saved record to preserve changes from another tab. Room changes/leave clear memory but keep the device cache. No content is persisted to an application server. **A new device entering an empty room cannot retrieve posts until a participant holding a copy connects. Clearing browser site data removes that browser's copy.** Cache failures are reported in the post list while live sharing remains available. The room retains up to 30 active posts, 1000 total records, 50 comments and 200 unique recent viewers per post. Deletion records discard media/comments/viewers, keeping only the small tombstone needed for synchronization. Conflicting full caches converge on the 30 newest posts.

Validation: `node scripts/check-map-posts.mjs` covers URL/content validation, author-only mutation, duplicate reaction delivery, undo, shared comments, concurrent/late-join snapshots, deduplicated authenticated viewers, deterministic full-cache merge and lightweight deletion tombstones. Local browser fixtures tested text creation, camera-relative placement/moving, remote comments/reactions, image compression/upload, deletion, player lifecycle and mobile dialog geometry. Drag/persistence fixtures additionally check precise mouse movement, touch movement, click suppression and restoration after reload. No live-room posts were created during QA; iframe source/lifecycle was checked with remote playback blocked.
